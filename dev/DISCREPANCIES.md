# Discrepancies between the papers and this implementation

A running log of places where a number in the papers cannot be reproduced from the papers' own formulae and parameters, or where our implementation knowingly differs from the papers' formulae. Each entry says what the paper prints, what we get, how we interpret the difference and how the validation gate treats it. Notation is HOS's. Gate IDs refer to `VALIDATION.md`; the full numbers are in its §4. Add new entries at the end; do not rewrite old ones when a later finding changes the interpretation, append to them.

## Paper values we cannot reproduce

### DR-1. HOS Table II, run 5: the Eq. 56 variance is printed as 1.0 but evaluates to 10.4

- **Where.** HOS p. 292, Table II, row 5 (f = 8.4, c² = 0.228, γ = 1.09), column "variance (56)".
- **Paper.** 1.0. The run's measured relative variance is 0.50. Footnote b: "Run 5 is outside the validity range of the asymptotic analysis of Section 4.5 because γ is so close to 1."
- **Ours.** Eq. 56, (γc² + γ − 1)/[4(γ − 1)²] = 0.33852/0.0324 = 10.45. Rows 1–4 reproduce from the same formula to the printed precision (0.042, 0.083, 0.175, 0.467 against 0.04, 0.08, 0.17, 0.47), and all five ⟨T_r⟩ values reproduce from Eq. 58 (11843, 483, 93.9, 35.4, 34.8 against 12000, 480, 94, 35, 35), so the formula and the parameters are right.
- **Checked.** The tracked text layer `resources/raw-text/hoyng-2001-gafd.txt` reads "1.0" for this cell, so the resource transcription is faithful. No PDF was opened. No reading of Eq. 56 gives 1.0; the exact-distribution dv over a > 0 for these parameters is 0.48, close to the run's 0.50.
- **Interpretation.** The parabolic approximation behind Eq. 56 has no meaning for γ this close to 1 (the well is barely there: a_m = 0.29), which the footnote says in other words. The printed 1.0 may be a placeholder or a slip; it is not a value of Eq. 56.
- **Gate.** T3.3 asserts rows 1–4 and records row 5. Status 2026-10-20: open, nothing further to do unless a later paper or erratum explains the cell.

## Known systematic differences between our simulation and the papers' formulae

### DR-3. D₀ of the binned noise is 5–6% below the δ-correlated Eq. 36

- **Where.** HOS Eq. 36 with the correlation function B.7, p. 284 and p. 309; our Tier 2 results, `VALIDATION.md` §4.
- **Paper.** D₀ = ℱ ∫ sin θ (P̃₀T₀)² dθ = 0.1136 for the standard run (ℱ = 3.22×10⁻², integral 3.529), i.e. (D₀τ_c)^½ = 0.075.
- **Ours.** The exact D₀ of F piecewise constant on N_c = 10 bins is τ_c f² Σ_k (∫_k w P̃₀T₀/√(2N_c sin θ))² = 0.1078 at N = 60 and 0.1063 at N = 240; measured directly from V₀₀ on the frozen b₀: 0.1087 ± 1.4%.
- **Interpretation.** Jensen's inequality: each squared bin integral is at most θ_c times the integral of the square, with equality only if P̃₀T₀/√sin θ were constant across the bin. Eq. 36 is the continuum limit N_c → ∞ at fixed f²τ_c/N_c². The papers' own simulations carry the same 5% (they used the same bins), so their measured D₁ and the fitted (c², γ) absorb it; it only matters when a theoretical D₀ is combined with a measured D₁.
- **Gate.** T2.3 passes on the measured value against the exact binned formula; the theory module uses Eq. 36 as the papers do. Status 2026-10-19: closed, a known systematic to quote when comparing simulation with theory.
- **Addendum (2026-10-20, T4.12).** The effect is large for the dynamics, not only for D₀: at the same f²τ_c/N_c², N_c = 20 (where the binned D₀ equals Eq. 36 to 0.1%) gives a 27% shorter ⟨T_r⟩ (75.9 ± 1.7 against 104.7 ± 2.8) and an 18% larger D₁ intercept than N_c = 10, because the overtones, with finer latitudinal structure, lose more of their forcing to the coarse bins than the dipole does. The f²τ_c/N_c² invariance of HOS Appendix E is therefore a continuum-limit statement, and every comparison with the papers must keep their N_c = 10, τ_c = 0.05 (`VALIDATION.md` §4, T4.12).

### DR-5. The intercept of the D₁ fit depends on how the bin averages are weighted

- **Where.** HOS Eq. 61 and Fig. 8 (p. 291–292): "histogram of bin averages, fitted curve 1.36 + 2.00a² for a ≤ 1.1"; our Tier 4 results, `VALIDATION.md` §4.
- **Paper.** D₁/ℱ ≃ 1.36 + 2.00a², hence c² = 0.246, γ = 2.96 (Eq. 63); the histogram "rises from ≈ 1.4 at a = 0" (figure read-off).
- **Ours.** Three seeds × 5×10⁴ τ_d pooled: an unweighted least-squares fit through the bin means for |a| ≤ 1.1 gives 1.46 + 2.08a²; weighting the bins by their counts gives 1.58 + 1.91a²; the direct mean of the samples with |a| ≤ 0.2 gives 1.24 ± 0.1. Hence c² = 0.261 (unweighted) or 0.291 (weighted) against 0.246; γ is 2.93 or 3.02 against 2.96 either way.
- **Interpretation.** D₁(a) is only approximately quadratic: the bin averages bend upward beyond |a| ≈ 0.8 (HOS note the a⁴ term beyond 1.1), so the intercept is an extrapolation whose value depends on the weighting. ~~The paper does not say how it fitted; a curve through the histogram is an unweighted fit, which is what the gate uses~~ (wrong, see the correction below). The physically relevant number, D₁(0) = Δ₁, is best estimated directly from the small-|a| bins once enough samples exist.
- **Correction (2026-10-20, step 7).** The Fig. 8 caption does state the procedure: "a least-square-fit of a quadratic function to the histogram levels, weighted by the number of points in the bin, for a ≤ 1.1" (text layer `resources/raw-text/hoyng-2001-gafd.txt`, line 1095; the resource file §5.1 has it too). The step-6 entry above misread this, and the gate it passed was not the paper's fit. With the paper's weighting our standard run gives 1.58 + 1.91a², an intercept 16% above 1.36, hence c² = 0.291 against 0.246 (18%) while γ = 3.02 against 2.96 and c₂ = 1.91 against 2.00 are within 5%. Because the weighting makes the fit a least-squares fit of the samples themselves, the excess means our D₁ near |a| ≈ 0.8–1.1, where the samples are, is 4–6% above the paper's curve (3.49 against 3.36 at a = 1), and the quadratic extrapolates the difference to the intercept. A slightly larger overtone content at given a is consistent with our ⟨T_r⟩ and variance both lying on the "more noise" side of the paper's (104.7 against 107, 0.164 against 0.16). Candidate causes: the integrator (HOS's explicit scheme damps the overtones differently from the exponential stepper at the same Δτ), the unspecified distribution of F, and the bin alignment; the convergence runs (T4.17) and the sweep (T4.7, below) separate these. The resource file's "histogram rises from ≈ 1.4 at a = 0" is a read-off of the histogram, not of the fit.
- **Gate.** `summarize` now reports the weighted fit as `d1_fit` (primary, the paper's procedure) with `d1_fit_unweighted` and `d1_zero` alongside. T4.3 (intercept) and T4.4 (c²) are recorded as known failures of the 10% gate with `@test_broken`, so the suite flags any change; c₂ and γ pass, and the unweighted fit is asserted for the record. Status 2026-10-20: open; see the sweep results in `VALIDATION.md` §4 for the f-dependence of the excess.
- **Sweep (2026-10-20).** The weighted intercept against the one the paper's (c², γ) imply: 1.57/1.22, 1.64/1.34, 1.58/1.36, 1.62/1.62, 1.92/1.99 for f = 4.4 … 8.4. At f ≥ 7.4, where the |a| distribution covers 0–1.1, weighted and unweighted fits agree with each other and with the paper to 2–5%; at f ≤ 6.4 the samples sit at |a| ≈ 0.7–1.1 and the intercept is an extrapolation with a lever arm of three to five. The D₁ bin averages themselves are close to the paper's curve where the samples are: 3.33 against 3.26 in the bin 0.95–1.0 at f = 6.4, 2%.
- **Cause, as far as it can be established (2026-10-20, `scripts/dr5_sensitivity.jl`).** Six variants of the standard run, three seeds × 5×10⁴ τ_d each, weighted intercept / D₁(1) bin / ⟨T_r⟩: baseline 1.58 / 3.33 / 104.7; UniformF 1.55 / 3.37 / 106.5; BinaryF 1.56 / 3.39 / 111.9; IMEX stepper at the same Δτ 1.84 / 3.43 / 98.3; exponential stepper at Δτ = 0.005 (same noise realisation) 1.57 / 3.32 / 106.9; N = 120 1.58 / 3.32 / 108.5. So (i) our value is converged in time step and grid to 1% (T4.17), (ii) the distribution of F is irrelevant, as HOS say of the "other details" (p. 272), and (iii) the time-integration scheme at Δτ = 0.01 moves the intercept by 0.26 and c² by 0.05, exactly the size of the gap, while moving ⟨T_r⟩ by 6% and dv by 0.007. The mechanism: any first-order scheme biases the overtone damping by O(|λ₁|Δτ) ≈ 3–6% per damping time (|λ₁| = 6.3 at C_m), which changes ⟨T₊²⟩ at given a by a few per cent and the extrapolated intercept by several times that; our IMEX under-damps and raises the intercept, a scheme that over-damps (backward Euler on the whole operator, say) would lower it toward the paper's. The exponential stepper is exact for the linear part and carries no such bias. HOS do not state their scheme (gap, `DEVELOPMENT.md` §1.2); an explicit one is ruled out by the diffusion operator's stability limit. The most probable reading is therefore that the paper's 1.36 includes its integrator's O(Δτ) bias on the overtones, and ours is the converged value; this cannot be confirmed without their scheme. The paper's ⟨T_r⟩ and variance are insensitive enough to the same bias (6%, 0.007) to be reproduced either way, which is what the sweep shows. Status: open as a reproduction gap, understood as a sensitivity.
- **SG-1, the integrator hypothesis tested (2026-10-24, `scripts/sg1_integrators.jl`, `VALIDATION.md` §4).** Backward Euler and Crank–Nicolson on the whole operator at Δτ = 0.01, each with the noise term explicit or implicit, beside the exponential stepper and the IMEX, at f = 6.4, 5.4 and 7.4, three seeds × 5×10⁴ τ_d. The second-order control (Crank–Nicolson, trapezoidal noise) reproduces the exponential stepper's intercept to 0.04 and ⟨T_r⟩ to 3%, so our value is converged across integrator families. The first-order schemes move the weighted intercept in both directions, +0.25 (IMEX, under-damping the first overtone by 12%), 0.00 (backward Euler with explicit noise, although it over-damps that mode by 7% and lowers D₁ at |a| ≈ 1 by 6%) and −0.14 (fully implicit backward Euler) at f = 6.4; but the only scheme that lowers it also lengthens ⟨T_r⟩ by 24% (129.5 ± 3.8 against the paper's 107 ± 5) because the implicit solve attenuates the noise injection, and it still stops at 1.45. No plausible implicit scheme reaches 1.36 with the paper's ⟨T_r⟩, at f = 5.4 either (1.54 at 704 ± 48 against 519 ± 53), and at f = 7.4 it would spoil the agreement that exists. The relative shape of the fit, c₂/c₀ ≈ 1.2 against the paper's 1.47, is the same for every scheme. The reading above, that the paper's 1.36 carries its integrator's bias, is therefore not supported; the gap is not an integrator effect. Remaining candidates: the fit procedure (the unweighted fit through the bin means is within 0.05–0.10 of the paper at all three forcings, the weighted one only at f = 7.4; the range |a| ≤ 1.1 and the paper's histogram binning are unknown in detail), the alignment of the noise bins with the grid, and the finite-difference forms of L and the shear term. Status: open as a reproduction gap; the integrator is excluded as its cause.

The aggregator's unit test now includes an interval hovering near zero. Status 2026-10-20: closed as a definition; the §2 text carries a note.

### DR-7. "𝒫 ≃ −1 between reversals" is a loose description: the median parity outside event windows is −0.82

- **Where.** HOS p. 276, after Eq. 28: "except during (aborted) reversals and excursions 𝒫 ≃ −1"; Fig. 2 (p. 275) shows 𝒫 "mostly near −1 with spikes to +1". `VALIDATION.md` T4.13 turned this into "median 𝒫 outside event windows < −0.9".
- **Ours (standard run, three seeds × 5×10⁴ τ_d, 𝒫 every n_c steps).** Masking every reversal, aborted reversal and excursion (the |a| < a_h windows of the Schmitt trigger, 14% of the run) leaves a median of −0.821, quartiles −0.924 and −0.617, a 10th percentile of −0.967 and 32% of the samples below −0.9; padding the windows by 1 τ_d (24% masked) gives −0.823; the whole run gives −0.811. The exclusion barely matters because the events are rare. Between them the symmetric part of the toroidal field carries E_S/E_A = (1 + 𝒫)/(1 − 𝒫) ≈ 0.10 of the energy, which is what the inter-event overtone level implies: the median |a₁| outside events is 0.21 (seed 1; |a₂| 0.14, |a| 0.85), and with the unit-energy modes of `DECISIONS.md` D4 the quadrupole alone gives 𝒫 ≈ (2|a₁|² − 1)/(2|a₁|² + 1) ≈ −0.83.
- **Interpretation.** HOS Fig. 2 shows the same |a₁| ≈ 0.1–0.3 between events, so their parity cannot have been much nearer −1 than ours; "≃ −1" reads the −1…1 axis of Fig. 2 loosely, and the −0.9 criterion was our own operationalisation. Even if the papers' overtone level were 16% lower than ours, as DR-5's integrator hypothesis would have it, their median would be about −0.84. The qualitative statement holds: the field is dipole-dominated between events and nearly pure dipole (𝒫 < −0.9) a third of the time.
- **Gate.** T4.13 (kind D) records the numbers; the test keeps `@test_broken median < −0.9` beside `@test` assertions that the median lies in (−1, −0.7), that the exclusion moves it by less than 0.05 and that the lower quartile is below −0.9. Status 2026-10-20: closed as a reading of the paper's wording; the resource file carries a Note.

### DR-8. Quenching by the total toroidal energy, or local quenching, with h = 1 − q runs away; the dipole statistics are not "unchanged"

- **Where.** HOS Section 3.3, p. 277 (resource §4.5): alternatives "tested with no material change in the dipole statistics": (1) α = α_m(1 − q) cos θ with q from the full toroidal energy (Eq. 18) instead of a₀² alone (Eq. 19); (2) α = α_m cos θ/(1 + q); (3) local instead of global quenching. Gate T4.16; `STRETCH_GOALS.md` SG-2 (2026-10-24).
- **Ours.** Implemented as `EnergyQuenching` (c = s(1 − E_T), E_T = Σ w T², the equilibrium b₀ unchanged), `InverseQuenching` (c = s(1 − a²)/(1 + s a²), equilibrium a = ±1, relaxation rate 2μ/(1 + s) = 0.883 instead of 1.526) and `LocalQuenching` (c_j = s − (1 + s) κ T_j², κ = 0.551 calibrated so that the equilibrium has a = 1; the equilibrium is antisymmetric but not the fundamental mode, ‖b − a b₀‖ = 0.68, toroidal energy 1.21). The energy and local laws, taken literally with h = 1 − q, diverge in the standard run: at f = 6.4 the three seeds of the energy law run away at τ = 475, ≈ 143 and 192, the local law at 402 and 116 (seeds 1, 2); at f = 5.4 at τ = 1556 (energy) and 558 (local). The papers' law and the inverse law never do (5×10⁴ τ_d, three seeds).
- **Mechanism.** The α-coefficient of the model equation is (1 + c) cos θ, and h = 1 − q lets it reverse sign when q > 1 (c < −1). The αΩ operator is mirror-symmetric under C → −C (its leading growth rate at C = −100 is Λ = 0.528, the same as at +100; the critical number is −C₀), so the α-reversed dynamo is as supercritical as the normal one. The papers' law reaches reversal only at |a| > √(1 + 1/s) = 1.54 (the standard run reaches |a| = 2.5 and a toroidal energy of 17 over 5×10⁴ τ_d), briefly, and the quenching ignores the overtones, so a relaxes before the mirror mode grows. The energy law reverses α whenever an overtone burst lifts E_T above 1 + 1/s = 2.37, which the standard run does routinely; the local law whenever κ T(θ)² > 1 somewhere. Once reversed, the growing field strengthens the reversal: in seed 3 of the energy law E_T goes 2.3 → 3.9 → 5.8 → 9 → 28 in 0.5 τ_d and overflows in the next. This is a property of the model as posed, not of the integrator (the exponential update is exact for frozen coefficients; the e-folding time of the runaway, 0.2 τ_d, is 20 steps).
- **Resolution for the gate.** The floored variants `floored = true` clamp c ≥ −1, i.e. the α-effect is suppressed to zero but never reversed, which is what "quenching" means physically; the floor is inactive at every equilibrium (`test/quenching.jl`) and the floored laws run stably. T4.16 compares the inverse law and the floored energy and local laws with the papers' law (`VALIDATION.md` §4); the unfloored divergence is a regression test. HOS do not say how their alternatives were implemented or how long they were run; with h = 1 − q and runs of the Table II length they would have met the runaway at f = 6.4 unless their q was bounded or their overtone bursts smaller, so their statement should be read as applying to a quenching that does not reverse α.
- **Result (`VALIDATION.md` §4, T4.16).** Even floored, laws (1) and (3) are not immaterial: at f = 6.4 the reversal rate is 2.9 and 3.2 times the papers' law's (⟨T_r⟩ 36.6 and 32.5 against 104.7 τ_d), at f = 5.4 five and seven times, at f = 7.4 twice, with variances 35–135% higher, because a quenching that counts the overtone energy or the local field switches α off during the bursts that drive reversals and so removes the dipole's restoring force when it is needed. Law (2) keeps the reversal rate (−3 to −6%) but raises the between-reversal variability by 40–50% (shallower well, same barrier). The floored papers' law agrees with the unfloored to 0.1% in ⟨T_r⟩ and 1–4% in the variances. HOS's statement is reproduced only for the reversal rate under law (2). Status 2026-10-24: open as a reading of the paper; gate T4.16 recorded as not met; the resource file carries a Note.
